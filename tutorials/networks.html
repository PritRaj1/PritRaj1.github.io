The mechanics of neural networks

Neural networks explained by their frameworks and a philosophical divide: functional purity vs object-oriented convenience.

Passing the Torch

I've learned a lot since my hard-line stances on deep learning frameworks, and it doesn't all hold, (PyTorch is really fast). That said, I still wouldn't advise someone to only learn PyTorch - a scout is always prepared.

Ignoring personal preference, it can be profoundly meaningful to explore deep learning in languages beyond what we're familiar with, because:

PyTorch abstracts away the fundamental nature of neural networks.

This stands in contrast to the compile-centric style of JAX and Julia's Lux.jl:

Torch torch.nn

quick and intuitive learning, backed by a vast and supportive ecosystem + the convenience of object-oriented design

JAX Flax NNX

familiarly object-oriented, (nnx api), but with native JIT/XLA compilation, and first-class multi-device support and scalability

Julia Lux.jl

to develop a comprehensive understanding of advanced scientific and numerical computing - purely immutable and functional in its design (similar what JAX's Flax.linen used to be)

Da Vinci differentiated himself as a true Renaissance man with one key characteristic: meticulous mechanistic understanding.

By revisiting the fundamentals of neural networks through the lens of Flax NNX and Julia Lux.jl, you walk a bold and decisive path that many in the field have discouraged me from treading.

So welcome aboard the crazy train buddy, my pleasure to accomodate the journey.

Neural networks as stateful functions

Neural networks are mathematical functions, where approximation is guaranteed by the Universal Approximation theorem:

For any \( \varepsilon \gt 0 \) and any continuous many-to-one function, \( f(\bm{x}): K \to \mathbb{R} \), defined on a compact subset of \(n\)-dim space, \( K \subset \mathbb{R}^n \), there exists a singe layer feedforward neural network, \( F(\bm{x}; \bm{\theta}) \), such that: \[ \vert F(\bm{x}; \bm{\theta}) - f(\bm{x}) \vert \lt \varepsilon \quad \forall \bm{x} \in K \] The approximating network function takes the form: \[ F(\bm{x}; \bm{\theta)} = \sum_{j=1}^M w_j \sigma(a_j \cdot x_j + b_j) = \bm{w} \cdot \sigma \left( \bm{a}\bm{x} + \bm{b} \right) \] With learnable \( n \)-dim vectors, \(\bm{\theta} = \{ \bm{w}, \bm{a}, \bm{b} \}\)
