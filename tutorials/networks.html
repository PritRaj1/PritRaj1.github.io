The purity of neural networks

Neural networks explained by their frameworks and a philosophical divide: functional purity vs object-oriented convenience.

Passing the Torch

I've learned a lot since my hard-line stances on deep learning frameworks, and it doesn't all hold. That said, I still wouldn't limit myself to PyTorch alone - a scout is always prepared.

Firstly, Torch is implicitly parameterised. A neural network's parameters are closely tied to the torch.nn class its defined by, which can be quite opaque to a compiler, (and maybe a performance engineer). Meaning something like torch.compile is limited in terms how deep its optimisations can go.

Python/PyTorch also follows an interpreted or eager execution model (line-by-line). Meaning it's dynamic and fast to compile/prototype with, but at the cost of interpreter overhead, (e.g. each iteration of a loop would accumulate slowdowns).

These trade-offs are usually worked around, but my biggest problem remains:

PyTorch abstracts away the fundamental nature of neural networks.

This stands in contrast to the nature of JAX and Julia's Lux.jl. And it can be profoundly meaningful to explore deep learning in languages beyond what we're familiar with:

Torch torch.nn

for quick and intuitive learning, backed by a vast and supportive ecosystem and the convenience of object-oriented design

JAX Flax NNX

familiarly object-oriented, but with native JIT/XLA compilation, and first-class multi-device support and scalability

Julia Lux.jl

for a comprehensive mathematical understanding in advanced scientific and numerical computing, with purely immutable and functional design
